## Android home screen widgets

An autolinked native module provides separate Next prayer and My Duas widgets. Prayer consumes accepted cached calculations independently of alerts. Duas scroll full bundled Arabic and change occasions/variants inside native RemoteViews. Each Dua widget retains independent choices. Settings has separate add actions; optional links open the exact invocation without waiting for offscreen prayer initialization. See [behavior, lifecycle and acceptance](ANDROID_WIDGETS.md).

The prayer widget adapts from a small next-prayer card to a five-column timetable or a narrow vertical agenda. Daily snapshots retain elapsed prayer times, sunrise, locally converted Hijri labels and midnight bounds in the calculation location's timezone. The next prayer gets a gold highlight and native countdown; larger sizes add a prominent summary, Gregorian date and location. Prayer and midnight alarms refresh the displayed day without JavaScript. Each instance retains its own dimensions. Its gradient and highlight resources do not change the accepted Dua widget.

## Startup and tab responsiveness

Bundled fonts and saved app settings load in parallel. First-time users still see onboarding before prayer-location work starts. Returning users, and users who finish onboarding, see the splash while the mounted prayer screen prepares its first usable frame. The overlay fades after current-location cached/fetched prayers or an explicit recovery state renders. Startup settings, location and prayer requests have time limits. The Hijri date uses the Calendar tab's immediate local conversion and cached API refinement, so the date boxes do not start blank.

After home is ready, idle callbacks preload other tabs one at a time through React Navigation without selecting them. Qibla sensors remain focus-scoped, and previously visited inactive screens freeze rendering. The prayer countdown skips hidden/background updates. A regular return to Prayer Times reloads preferences but reuses prayer data for five minutes; changed location/calculation settings, missing today's prayers and app resume trigger a refresh. Notification preferences use a single storage batch. This removes known repeat work, but responsiveness still requires Omar's preview-build review. See [decision](DECISIONS.md#reveal-a-prepared-prayer-home-and-reduce-tab-switch-work--2026-09-07).

The tour starts with language, then follows the original welcome, Prayer Times, Quran, Tasbeeh/Duas, Hadith/Calendar and charity sequence. Existing location preferences load before interaction is enabled.

## Prayer times and alerts

The Prayer Times home card uses the existing next-prayer calculation to select one of five vector backgrounds. Its sun or moon position is fixed for that prayer, while the centered countdown remains live and updates once per second when the tab is active. The mosque silhouette and curved layers fill an absolute native background view, clipped to the card's rounded edges. Only the centered foreground text receives horizontal padding, so the art cannot push the countdown out of the card. Separate light and dark palettes preserve contrast. The card height decreases on shorter viewports so all five prayer rows retain space. This is a visual cue for the next prayer, not a live solar-position calculation. See [decision](DECISIONS.md#put-the-next-prayer-countdown-over-time-specific-mosque-art--2026-09-23).

Prayer calculation settings open as a full-screen page with a back header and safe-area/keyboard handling. The fields scroll independently of Save; wider screens bound the form width to 720 points. Country/method search remains inside the same page; Back returns from a picker to the form before closing the page. Closing discards unsaved edits, and saving persists them through the existing service. This supersedes the earlier half-screen request at Omar's direction; the smaller sound/reminder sheet is unchanged. GPS/manual city, regional method suggestion, explicit method (including custom angles), Asr school, high-latitude rule and per-prayer minute adjustments affect the actual AlAdhan request and cache identity. The zero-valued Shia Ithna-Ashari method is preserved. 12/24-hour formatting and optional Imsak beneath Fajr affect display only.

`savePrayerTimeSettings` validates coordinates/city, angles and integer minute offsets before requesting replacement days. On fetch failure the old persisted settings and alerts remain intact; the sheet stays open with a retryable error. After fetching it persists settings and then schedules matching times. Both prayer home and the main Settings entry use this service; the older Settings path that cancelled alerts before fetching has been removed. Notification failure is reported separately with a retry action on either entry page. Settings passes the selected app language into the form. Display-only saves need no network or rescheduling. These settings-save guarantees do not imply a new device delivery test. AlAdhan's [calculation documentation](https://aladhan.com/calculation-methods) defines the tuning order; [method metadata](https://api.aladhan.com/v1/methods) supplies the supported IDs. Read-only requests for New York on 2026-09-19 verified standard/Hanafi Asr, custom angles, and all six offsets.

Automatic location uses a shared, bounded GPS request and a cached neighborhood/city label. Users can choose a different city by tapping the location. AlAdhan monthly calendars are cached separately for each location and calculation method; today's times appear as soon as their response arrives. Changing calculation settings invalidates the previous schedule.

Permissions are introduced during onboarding and managed in Settings → Adhan & notifications. They do not occupy the prayer home page. Android alarms are persisted in the local `prayer-alarm` module and scheduled through AlarmManager. At delivery time, bundled recordings play through a foreground service with a Stop action. This does not require Metro or a JavaScript timer. Default, Silent and Vibrate alerts use distinct notification channels; Silent disables sound and vibration, while Vibrate requests vibration without sound. None skips that prayer's main alert. Optional advance reminders are independent, including when the main prayer is None. The preference sheet reads the next reminder timestamp from the persisted native schedule; a selected reminder alone is not proof of delivery.

Prayer alerts and Adhan are local alarms, so they need no Firebase/FCM project configuration. The preview profile contains the native alarm module and recordings. Delivery requires notifications plus Android's Alarms & reminders access; sound also follows system volume, silent/DND and channel settings. Force-stopping the app prevents delivery until it is opened again. The rolling schedule covers up to 30 days and refreshes on app use/background work, so it is not an indefinite guarantee after the last open. Earlier Samsung tests verified the sound modes and Stop; see [evidence and limits](ANDROID_PREVIEW.md). No advertising SDK is currently configured.

The alarm and notification serve different roles: AlarmManager triggers the app's receiver at prayer time, the notification supplies the visible prayer alert, and a foreground playback service plays a full bundled Adhan with a Stop action. Alarms & reminders is Android's scheduling permission; this implementation does not create entries in the phone's Clock app. Android documents this separation in its [alarm scheduling guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms).

Default playback follows notification volume, ringer and DND settings. The opt-in **Play Adhan during DND** switch uses Android alarm audio/volume and the user's DND alarm exception, including when the ringer is silent. It does not change global DND or volume. Total silence or a DND policy that blocks alarms still suppresses playback. An active MediaSession and scoped volume-change monitoring silence the current Azan when volume decreases; the foreground Activity also handles Volume Down directly. The Stop notification action remains. These new native paths require an updated APK and await device verification.


## Reading and remembrance

Created Duas have a trash button in My Duas and a Delete action in their reader options. Both use an English/Arabic confirmation naming the item. Confirming removes that custom Dua, its bookmark and its personal note through the saved-Dua persistence queue; the reducer checks the stored custom flag so this action cannot delete catalogue Duas. Deleting from the reader returns to the saved list, avoiding a stale reading snapshot. Unbookmarking a custom Dua remains a separate action and preserves its text. My Duas displays storage failures so an unsuccessful disk save is visible.

All three Quran layouts share ayah actions. Holding an ayah or word opens listening and Tafsir choices; the ayah-list ellipsis opens the same actions. Tafsir has a remembered Arabic/English source selector, publisher attribution, passage grouping, mapped Duri references, retry and bounded recent offline storage. See [Tafsir behavior and sources](QURAN_TAFSIR.md).

Dua categories and everyday shortcuts use locally bundled, theme-colored Hugeicons and two original symbols, with occasion-specific imagery and distinct entering/leaving badges. Dua subjects are distinct from the bottom navigation: seated/prostrating worshippers, an ihram pilgrim and a heart/shield replace repeated beads, prayer rug, Kaaba and Dua hands. Their navigation targets and Hisn text are unchanged. See [icon mapping and license](DUA_ICONS.md).

The Surah-title picker and Quran-library list button show Juz, Surah and Ayah wheels together, followed by one Done action. Juz changes choose its native starting Surah/ayah; Surah changes choose its first ayah; ayah changes update Juz. The columns roll with native snapping, curved perspective and throttled selection haptics. Linked columns animate without remounting the Ayah range; reduced-motion preference is respected. Draft choices remain in the picker until Done; dismissal leaves reading unchanged. Choosing a destination resolves its matching printed page or text anchor, stops old audio and highlights the chosen ayah. Exact native positions are persisted alongside canonical keys to retain split Duri references. Flow paragraphs can start at the selected ayah, and viewability retains that anchor in short Surahs until manual interaction or audio following. Page entry remains in reading options and accepts Arabic/Persian digits. See [source, scroll safety and navigation details](QURAN_NAVIGATION.md).

The Quran library and reader have separate screens. Mushaf renders published vector outlines with the original calligraphy and line breaks. Each Hafs/Duri page is a compressed offline asset; the decoded cache holds at most five pages and the animated pager retains three. A separate precisely scaled ayah layer provides deliberate long-hold listening and highlighting without re-parsing the artwork during playback. Short taps and moved holds do not select. Mushaf always fits the complete printed page; it has no zoom or font-size buttons. Actual letter resizing/reflow is available in the two text layouts. Horizontal swipes remain right-next/left-previous. Fixed session-relative slots and one native-driven page-position value replace the rebase/reset handoff that could flash overlapping pages; each scene keeps its own clipped viewport and touch frame. The printed Duri edition stays distinct from Hafs; its Al-Mulk 9–10 region pair maps to one existing native audio passage. See [printed-page sources and limits](QURAN_VECTOR_PAGES.md).

Flowing text and ayah-by-ayah read the whole selected Surah continuously. `QuranTextPager` mounts the current Surah first, then retains adjacent lists during idle time and prepares the adjacent reading positions before allowing a slide. Simultaneous scroll/pan handlers measure pulls at either boundary: 64 points beyond the boundary arms a transition. There are no persistent gesture cards or instruction labels. A small themed arrow forms only during the overscroll, then fades independently of page movement. Pull down for the previous Surah's end, or up for the next Surah's beginning. Both lists move together through one clipped viewport using chapter coordinates relative to the session's fixed origin. Committing the new chapter changes ownership without repositioning the lists or resetting the completed animation. Events carry their source chapter so late cancellation from the outgoing list cannot rebound the destination; spring-back is reserved for an unfinished pull. Screen-reader navigation actions remain available without visible instructions. First cells are measured before seeking a saved/previous endpoint so native viewability can report readiness. List identity changes with font size, translation, riwayah, width and system font scale so offscreen cached heights cannot survive a text reflow. The current ayah remains the restoration anchor; the list is revealed after that position is ready. Memoized rows keep ordinary measurement in refs; only the followed row requests a batched audio-position update. Pull gestures require the actual first/last row to be visible and measured, and ordinary scrolling does not start a return spring. See [scroll investigation](QURAN_TEXT_SCROLL.md). Restoration retries stop when the anchor becomes visible or manual interaction starts. Native copy selection is disabled in Quran, Dua and Hadith; Quran long holds deliberately select a word for listening. Editable fields retain normal editing. See the [transition decision](DECISIONS.md#keep-surah-text-mounted-throughout-vertical-transitions--2026-09-07).

Dua Discover keeps nine broad topics and adds twelve Everyday shortcuts to existing Hisn occasions. Six common shortcuts are visible first (leaving/entering home, entering/leaving the mosque, after prayer and before sleep); Show all reveals waking, meals, restroom and after-wudu shortcuts. One tap opens the actual Dua and retains its related variants. Morning/evening counting routines remain separate. Topics open a searchable spring sheet of named occasions with an Arabic preview; choosing a search result opens its matching card after the sheet finishes closing. Read the whole category retains continuous topic reading and its occasion picker. Global search, saving and optional notes remain. Saved items keep compact category metadata, preserving existing personal content.

The Hisn catalogue contains 133 reading categories covering the source's 132 chapters; morning and evening are separate. Arabic is offline, while English API meanings are cached on first use. Timed Athkar save counts per local date and period, and advance after the source's repetition target. See [content provenance](HISN_CONTENT.md).

Tasbih counts and goals are saved per dhikr. The entire selection card opens a searchable sheet with separate Adhkar and Collections choices. Collections combine existing or custom words, ordered entries and individual targets. Presets include After Prayer (33/33/33/1) and five short Morning Dhikr selections from Hisn; the complete morning reading sequence remains in Duas. Collection rounds save their own count array and current step, advance after a 300 ms target pause, and resume after leaving or reopening. They do not overwrite standalone counts or goals. Editing a collection starts a fresh round; deleting a custom collection retains its Adhkar in the library. State migrates from `tasbih:v2` to `tasbih:v3` after successful hydration and uses serialized writes. Counting occupies a separate area so it cannot intercept the selection card's touches.

## Layout and calendar

The prayer page reserves space above the floating navigation, uses centered date boxes and distributes remaining height across the prayer rows without scrolling. Shared option sheets have a transparent backdrop and a tappable 44-point handle area around a small line, without an X. Panels reveal upward and fold back down using the same spring as the Quran player, with their bottom edge anchored. Handle dragging adjusts the revealed height; short pulls restore it and deliberate drags/flicks in either direction dismiss directly from the current reveal. No whole-panel translation or entrance/exit fade is used. Content retains its measured full layout inside the changing clip, avoiding per-frame text/list reflow. Exit completes before parent dismissal, with interrupted completions invalidated. The inline Quran player uses the same handle. See [panel behavior](SHEET_INTERACTIONS.md). Adhan choices remain half the viewport height. Language and reading-layout choices use the same sheet; reciters are chosen inside the expanding player. Settings shows “Manarat al-Muslim” (منارة المسلم in Arabic) as its bottom footer without a dividing border, following Omar’s naming correction.

Calendar months render immediately from local Umm al-Qura conversion, then refresh from AlAdhan. Memory and 24-hour disk caches retain month data and deduplicate concurrent requests; adjacent months are prefetched. The grid stays at six rows. Selecting a date does not reload its month, and background failures leave the dates visible.

Named Islamic occasions tint their grid cell and add a dot; selecting one shows its name. Upcoming events scan the same cached/local calendar rows across fourteen Gregorian months, yielding between months to avoid blocking initial touches. Counts use local civil dates rather than elapsed 24-hour periods, retaining today and handling DST/year rollover. A midnight/app-resume hook refreshes Today/Tomorrow/In N days. Loading corrected month data refreshes the list as well as the grid. Local moon-sighting caveats remain visible.

Hadith collections load once per session, with bounded requests. Arabic and English entries join by Hadith number. Search treats user input literally and normalizes Arabic diacritics.

## Appearance and compass

Appearance defaults to the device setting and follows live system changes. Settings also permits explicit Light/Dark overrides. Interactive Mushaf uses vector ink that inherits theme color. Compass cardinal artwork uses an SVG color filter in dark mode. The Kaaba marker uses fixed black/gold vector shapes matching its existing silhouette and never passes through this filter. The eight navigation icons use the Hugeicons free rounded stroke family, including prayer rug, Quran, Tasbih, Dua, Kaaba and Ramadan calendar symbols; tab names are unchanged. The MIT notice is in `assets/licenses/hugeicons.txt`.

Qibla keeps the original artwork. Cardinal directions follow north; the Kaaba marker follows the location's Qibla bearing independently. Sustained low heading accuracy prompts calibration. An animated figure-eight guides movement; three or more high-accuracy readings spanning at least 2.5 seconds enable Done. A timer alone cannot complete calibration.

The September 7 follow-up source changes are awaiting user testing; the previously completed APKs do not include them.

## Immediate settings and Quran audio

Sound choices stay enabled while saved preferences load. A preference model immediately displays edited fields, merges untouched saved fields, and protects accepted choices from late hydration or sheet closure. Errors stay visible and retryable. Settings language feedback is local/immediate, global language changes use a React transition, and stored writes remain ordered. Retained inactive tab children avoid re-rendering heavy screens on each language change. Shared sheets now use an anchored spring reveal after Omar explicitly rejected fading/translated motion. Native window readiness and measured size gate the initial reveal; controls remain enabled. Reduced-motion preference skips the animation. Quran layout radio buttons edit a small draft; Continue closes the sheet before the new layout is rendered.

All Quran layouts provide continuous listening with seven readers, including Sudanese Alzain and Noreen. Noreen displays Duri text and native ayah numbering; other readers display Hafs. Hold a word in text layouts for word-only, ayah-only or continuous listening. In Mushaf, hold a printed ayah for ayah/continuous listening or choose one of its words, then explicitly play it. Word choices use the exact matching-reader character ranges; they do not invent word touch regions on the printed artwork. A floating bar provides play/pause. Its arrow and summary expand the same card upward for all seven reader choices, skip, stop and following; selecting a reader stays in that card. The card animates its dimensions while its inset circular play button stays fixed in size and its footer remains at the same vertical position. A dismissing handle drag continues directly to compact height without dipping or springing back first. Outside tap, Android Back, the arrow and the draggable top handle collapse its options without changing playback. Player and reader selection use no Modal; the deliberate held-word/ayah action sheet remains separate. In Mushaf it shares the bottom navigation space below the page ink. Text layouts include bottom scroll padding and adjusted follow bounds so the final words clear the pill. Inline +/- buttons adjust their text size. Manual scrolling turns following off until Back to recitation; measured native text lines follow the last spoken word without jumping backward during silence, while Mushaf follows/highlights whole ayahs using its own edition index. Repeated passages use time-ordered word cues even when native ayah numbering moves backward in the recording. Conservative word clips cannot fabricate a boundary through a multiword or missing interval.

All timing metadata are local and lazily loaded per chapter. Sudanese playback retains one native Surah player while other readers prefetch one MP3 ahead. Source timing recovery restores ayah playback separately from word highlights. First audio remains network-dependent. Canonical stored reading references convert to native Duri through word routes, not equal ayah numbers. English ayah translations are not shown against unmatched Duri boundaries. [Quran audio](QURAN_AUDIO.md) records provenance, reproduction, source audits, native-reference coverage and the distinction between structural and acoustic validation.

The Azan library contains eight recordings. Five additions (one local Sudanese recording and four researched regional recordings) have full Android audio and short notification clips. [Audio provenance](ADHAN_AUDIO.md) records attribution and hashes. An old native APK rejects the newly added sounds/Vibrate with an update message before replacing a working schedule.
